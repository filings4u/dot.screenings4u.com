          <figure class="article-feature-image"><img src="assets/img/platform-drivers.webp" alt="Covered worker workspace"><figcaption>Example screenings4u DOT workspace used to support the operational workflow described in this article.</figcaption></figure>
          <p class="article-intro">This article focuses on how to organize the work inside a DOT program. It is operational guidance for using a structured workforce and compliance system; it is not a substitute for the rules or official agency guidance that apply to your organization.</p>
          <section class="article-section"><h2>Agency assignment comes first</h2><p>The same employer can have very different compliance workflows depending on the transportation work being performed. Capturing the applicable DOT agency at the employer and worker level makes downstream configuration more precise.</p></section>
<section class="article-section"><h2>Use the agency's own workforce language</h2><p>A trucking company may think in terms of drivers, while an aviation employer may think in terms of flight crews, maintenance personnel, dispatchers, or other covered functions. Using the correct workforce terminology makes the portal easier for the customer to understand.</p></section>
<section class="article-section"><h2>Keep positions structured</h2><p>A structured position catalog is more useful than a free-text job-title field alone. The organization can still store the company's internal job title, but the regulated position or covered function should also be represented in a consistent way.</p></section>
<section class="article-section"><h2>Configure programs and pools from the agency context</h2><p>Programs, random pools, selections, and testing workflows should inherit the agency context rather than asking administrators to recreate it on every transaction.</p></section>
<section class="article-section"><h2>Keep agency content separate from legal interpretation</h2><p>Operational software can organize records, workflows, and agency-specific terminology. Regulatory applicability and legal interpretation should still be confirmed against the rules and official guidance that apply to the organization.</p></section>
          <div class="blog-note"><strong>Operational note:</strong> Keep the software record aligned with your actual program procedures. When a regulatory question affects whether testing is required, who is covered, or how an event must be handled, confirm the requirement against the applicable rule and official DOT or agency guidance.</div>
