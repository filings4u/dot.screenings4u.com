USCG REGULATORY OVERVIEW

Marine employers have Part 16 program duties and separate serious-marine-incident obligations.

Coast Guard chemical-testing requirements are primarily found in 46 CFR Part 16, with serious marine incident testing addressed in 46 CFR Part 4. DOT drug-testing procedures in 49 CFR Part 40 also apply to Coast Guard-regulated drug testing.

PART 16

Covered mariners and marine employers

Part 16 applies to specified crewmembers serving on U.S.-flag commercial vessels. Marine employers are responsible for administering required drug and alcohol testing programs for covered employees.

TESTING TYPES

Pre-employment, random, reasonable cause, and more

Coast Guard guidance describes pre-employment, random, reasonable-cause, periodic, and post-casualty testing. Periodic testing is generally the individual mariner’s responsibility, while marine employers administer the employer program.

SERIOUS MARINE INCIDENTS

Part 4 creates a separate casualty workflow

Persons directly involved in a serious marine incident may be subject to drug and alcohol testing under Part 4, which requires a distinct event record and timely testing workflow.

EDUCATION & RECORDS

Training, reporting, and recordkeeping

Marine employers have employee-assistance/education duties and must maintain testing records and required reports. Coast Guard DAPI inspections can review recordkeeping, collections, MRO activity, testing, and program administration.

Important: This overview is educational and operational, not legal advice. Use the linked agency sources and the current regulations for official requirements and interpretations.
