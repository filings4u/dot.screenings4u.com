Pools & Random Selections

Keep Random Pool Eligibility Current Before You Run a Selection

A cleaner random workflow starts before the selection button is clicked. Keep eligibility, program enrollment, and pool membership aligned.

September 21, 20266 minute readEmployers & C/TPAsscreenings4u DOT Team

This article focuses on how to organize the work inside a DOT program. It is operational guidance for using a structured workforce and compliance system; it is not a substitute for the rules or official agency guidance that apply to your organization.

Treat the pool as a live population

A random pool should represent the people who are currently eligible for the program being administered. That means the roster should change as the workforce changes instead of being treated like a static annual list.

Resolve workforce changes first

Before a selection, review new hires, separations, transfers, leave status, agency assignment, and program enrollment. Correcting those records before the event is cleaner than explaining later why someone appeared in—or was missing from—the selection population.

Separate eligibility from selection history

Current pool membership and historical selection activity are different records. A worker can leave a pool today without erasing the fact that the person was eligible or selected during a previous period. Preserving both views helps administrators maintain a useful operational history.

Document the event, not just the names

A useful selection record should identify the pool, date, population size, selected members, test types, and status of the event. That creates a repeatable record that can be reviewed later without reconstructing the event from emails or spreadsheets.

Use follow-up work queues

After the event is created, administrators still need to notify people, create testing orders, track collection status, and resolve exceptions. A selection process is more useful when those follow-up tasks are connected to the event itself.

Operational note: Keep the software record aligned with your actual program procedures. When a regulatory question affects whether testing is required, who is covered, or how an event must be handled, confirm the requirement against the applicable rule and official DOT or agency guidance.
